# Multi-step Causal Chains

## Why include them

Many risks do not come from the original action alone. A small disturbance changes attention, posture, route, timing, or trust, and a second event then causes the actual harm. Include some negative scenarios that show this chain so users can identify an early break point instead of focusing only on the first trigger.

## Required coverage

- In a simple table, include at least **one** negative scenario whose event description contains a two- or three-step causal chain.
- In a detailed HTML page, include a meaningful spread of chain scenarios across different domains. Do not force every negative item into a chain.
- Prefer chains involving attention, avoidance, delay, trust, fatigue, or a small workaround, because these are plausible and actionable.
- A chain must not imply that a disabled person, a bystander, or a protected group is responsible for harm.

## Chain construction

Use two to four links. Each link must be plausible on its own:

1. **Initial condition**: a mundane event or choice.
2. **Attention or behavior change**: the person looks up, steps aside, delays, improvises, or trusts an unexpected contact.
3. **Second event**: an independent hazard now becomes reachable.
4. **Consequence**: injury, loss, account compromise, legal exposure, or disruption.

Mark uncertainty and conditions in the evidence field. Do not present the chain as inevitable. The probability is for the whole chain, not for the first link alone.

## Example shape

Negative scenario: “为避开路面积水而抬头看上方，未注意脚下台阶，后退时被后方车辆或热饮摊绊倒受伤。”

Better compact wording:

> “避水抬头看上方时忽略台阶，后退一步撞上后方热饮摊，造成烫伤。”

Evidence: “地面积水、台阶和后方摊位同时存在时，注意力转移会叠加风险。”

Action: “先停下确认落脚点，再绕行；不要边抬头边后退。”

The chain is useful because the first event is small and the final harm is preventable by choosing where to stop.

## Style rules

- Use concrete links in the event text: “因……而……随后……” or “为……转而……结果……”. Avoid vague “引发连锁反应”.
- Keep the category to the **final direct harm**, not every link. One category only.
- If the chain involves a serious injury, state the condition and avoidance step without graphic detail.
- Do not create absurd Rube Goldberg sequences. Each link must have a believable mechanism and a plausible environmental condition.
- Prefer early break points in the action column: stop moving, check footing, verify through an official channel, save work, ask someone, or delay a decision.
- The chain scenario still follows the normal probability ordering and the negative premium of 20%–35% over positive count.
